Logicard
Brand Book

How Logicard looks, sounds, and decides

The reference for staying recognisably Logicard as the site grows — new category pages, the weekly quiz, brand partnerships, and whoever works on this next.

01

Who this is for

Every design and copy decision gets checked against this list first.

Primary audience

~2.8 million UK logistics workers — HGV drivers, warehouse and fulfilment staff, transport planners, fleet management, freight and ports, plus retired members of the industry. Often reading mid-shift, tired, on a phone.

Secondary audience

Brands and advertisers who want to reach that audience through Logicard — sponsorship, partner offers, workforce-recognition partnerships.

Product surface: £10/year membership (FREE promo for year 1) · workforce wellbeing content · employer workforce-recognition partnerships · a weekly 10-question quiz paying cashback for a perfect score.

02

Voice and tone

Three traits, one voice: direct, warm, and allergic to corporate speak. Write like a colleague who happens to know where the good deals are — not a marketing department.

01

Direct

Lead with the number, the saving, the deadline. Personality comes after clarity, not instead of it.

02

Warm, not saccharine

Acknowledge the graft without being patronising. "For the people who keep Britain moving" is the reference point.

03

No corporate jargon

No "synergy," "leverage," "solutions." If a line would fit in a corporate all-hands deck, rewrite it.

One voice, not two registers

Brand-facing copy trades warmth for credibility — real numbers, real reach ("2.8 million UK logistics workers," not vague enthusiasm) — but it isn't a different personality. If member-facing and brand-facing copy start reading like two different companies, that's the signal something has drifted.

The quiz is the one deliberate exception

A lighter, competitive, watercooler feature — it can carry more energy than a discount page: "Reckon you know your stuff? Nail all 10, walk away with cash." Still no fluff, just more cheek.

The failure mode to actively avoid

A "geezer" / white-van-man caricature. The workforce is far broader than that stereotype — warehouse staff, ops, admin, retired members, women in logistics — and a caricatured voice reads as patronising to most of the 2.8 million rather than authentic to any of it.

Sounds like Logicard

"£10 a year. Use code FREE and your first year's on us."

Doesn't sound like Logicard

"Unlock a world of exclusive savings tailored to your on-the-road lifestyle, mate!"

Iconography — line icons, 24×24 viewbox, 1.8–2px stroke, round caps, no fill.

Known failure mode: an icon using unstyled stroke="currentColor" inside a white/light card will inherit whatever text colour the page sets — this shipped invisible once already when the site's body text turned white. Always set an explicit stroke colour on icons inside light-background cards.

04

The Rules Scale

Not every rule carries the same weight. Use this to judge how much justification a change needs — and when to stop and ask the site owner first, rather than deciding alone.

🔒Tier 1 — Locked

Requires explicit sign-off before changing. Never change these as a side effect of an unrelated task.

  • The colour palette hex values
  • The logo lockup (colours, "Logi"/"card" split, pill treatment)
  • The core voice traits — direct, warm, no corporate jargon, no caricature
  • The £10/year price point and the FREE promo mechanic, wherever stated
  • Legal and compliance copy — Privacy Policy, Terms & Conditions, the complaints process, consent wording
🟡Tier 2 — Guided

Can change, but the change should be deliberate and explainable — flag the reasoning, don't change it quietly.

  • Page layouts and new page structures
  • New icons (must match the existing line-icon style)
  • Navigation structure — adding or removing nav items, dropdowns
  • Tone register for a genuinely new feature (e.g. establishing the quiz's voice)
  • New category pages and what they link to
🟢Tier 3 — Flexible

Encouraged to iterate freely, no sign-off needed.

  • Individual card copy/descriptions within an established template
  • Weekly quiz question content
  • Seasonal promotional copy (e.g. the monthly giveaway prize)
  • Marketing email subject lines
  • Minor spacing/padding adjustments within an existing component

When in doubt about which tier something falls into, treat it as the higher tier.

05

Using this document

Check whether the change touches a Tier 1 item. If so, confirm with the site owner before proceeding — even if the request seems small.

If it's Tier 2, make the change, but say what changed and why, so it's easy to review.

If it's Tier 3, proceed freely.

If a change reveals this document is wrong or out of date, update it in the same piece of work — it should always describe the site as it actually is.

Our Founders

Why We Built Logicard

Before Logicard, our founders worked in Logistics.

It was there that we began to understand something that has stayed with us ever since.

Whenever they spoke to another HGV driver, the message was remarkably similar.

“We are punished for working hard.”

“We are scrutinised over everything.”

“We are earning less, while everything costs more.”

“There is nothing out there for Logistics workers.”

These weren't the words of people who didn't want to work.

They were the words of people who were working incredibly hard — often under enormous pressure — and feeling that the industry they kept moving wasn't giving much back to them.

The People Behind the Loads

Working in Logistics opened our eyes to just how much is expected from the people behind the movement of goods.

HGV drivers can spend most of their working week on the road, away from their families and their normal lives.

They deal with traffic, deadlines, congestion, difficult deliveries, unpredictable conditions and the enormous responsibility that comes with driving a vehicle that can weigh up to 44 tonnes in Great Britain, depending on the vehicle and applicable weight limits.

Some drivers even referred to their HGVs as “death machines.”

Not because they wanted to cause harm.

Quite the opposite.

They understood the responsibility they carried.

One mistake involving a vehicle of that size can have devastating consequences.

Yet despite that responsibility, many drivers were telling us that their wages had fallen since COVID, while the cost of living continued to rise.

At the same time, agencies were competing against each other on price, creating what many workers saw as a race to the bottom.

We also saw the growing use of LTD drivers and different employment arrangements. For workers, this could mean fewer of the protections and benefits associated with traditional employment, including holiday pay in circumstances where they were genuinely self-employed.

The people doing some of the hardest and most responsible work were increasingly asking:

“What are we actually getting back?”

And It Isn't Just Drivers

The challenges don't stop when the truck reaches the warehouse.

Warehouse workers face their own pressures.

Long shifts.

Productivity targets.

Repetitive work.

Constant scrutiny.

And sometimes even the simplest things become difficult.

Getting to the toilet.

Getting to the canteen.

Having enough time to eat.

Then getting back to the working position before you're expected to start again.

A legal minimum break isn't necessarily the same thing as a meaningful opportunity to recover.

And while a driver can experience the loneliness of being on the road, a warehouse worker can experience something different — being surrounded by people but still feeling like nobody has time to listen.

Different jobs.

Different pressures.

The same underlying problem.

The people who keep Logistics moving were feeling increasingly disconnected from the industry around them.

Logistics Keeps Britain Moving

That stayed with us.

Because Logistics is essential.

Every day, thousands upon thousands of people get behind the wheel, walk onto warehouse floors, load vehicles, unload deliveries, pick orders, move stock and keep supply chains functioning.

They don't always get recognition.

But without them, the country stops.

The shelves don't get stocked.

The deliveries don't arrive.

The warehouses don't move.

Businesses don't operate.

Logistics workers keep Britain moving.

And we began to believe that the industry needed to give something back.

That's Where Logicard Began

Logicard was born from that idea.

Not from a boardroom.

Not from a marketing exercise.

From conversations with the people actually doing the work.

We wanted to create something that recognised Logistics workers as people — not simply as numbers, labour, drivers, pickers, loaders or delivery targets.

Something that could create a stronger sense of community.

Something that could make people feel valued.

Something that said:

“We see you.”

“We appreciate what you do.”

“You deserve something back.”

That is what Logicard is about.

The Future of Logistics Must Include Its People

We believe the future of Logistics cannot simply be about moving more goods, faster and cheaper.

It has to be about the people who make that possible.

Because if we continue asking Logistics workers to give more while receiving less, eventually we have to ask ourselves what kind of industry we are building.

Logicard is our answer to that question.

We want to build something that belongs to the people who keep the country moving.

Something that recognises their contribution.

Something that connects them.

Something that gives them real value.

Because after everything Logistics workers give to the industry…

It's time something was given back.
